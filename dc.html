Project Scope

Design a health & wellness app end to end — onboarding and account setup, a home dashboard that summarises today's meal, workout and mood, a workout library with guided sessions, a full diet-plan system (multiple named programs, approved/non-approved foods, weekly meal plans and recipes), a nutrition diary with food search and macro tracking, a meditation library, and FitGenie, an AI assistant that identifies a meal from a photo and returns its nutrition.

A note on context: this is a self-directed redesign, not a shipped or client product — I used it to practice unifying several habit-tracking domains (fitness, food, sleep, mind) into one coherent information architecture, end to end from onboarding through a full design system.

My role spanned the whole product: UX — the dashboard hierarchy, the diet-plan structure, and the food-logging flow; UI — every screen; and the design system — cards, progress rings, macro bars and list patterns reused across fitness, food and mindfulness.

Problem Statement

Most people trying to build healthier habits end up with three or four separate apps — one for workouts, one for calorie counting, one for meditation — each with its own login, its own streaks, and no shared picture of the day. Diet programs in particular tend to dump a wall of rules on day one (approved foods, banned foods, a meal plan) with no easy way to check them again once you're standing in a kitchen.

Goal: one dashboard for the whole day — today's meal, today's workout and today's calm in one place, with the diet rules always one tap away instead of memorised on day one.

Research & Ideation

I mapped the habits DC needed to support — eating, moving, resting, staying calm — and looked at where single-purpose apps make each one harder than it needs to be. Three decisions followed.

The dashboard is the daily brief, not a menu — home opens on today's diet summary, today's upcoming meal and today's exercise routine, so the plan for the day is visible before any navigation. Diet rules live where you'll actually need them — approved foods, non-approved foods and the meal plan are each their own screen inside the diet plan, not a one-time onboarding dump. Logging is conversational, not just a form — FitGenie lets you point a camera at a meal and get calories and macros back in a chat, instead of searching a food database by hand.

The core decision

Answer "what's my day look like" before asking the user to open anything else.

Diet, exercise and calm on one home screen  ·  diet rules always one tap away

Design & Results

FitZone turns each workout into a guided session — a looping demo video, a timer, and a numbered list of exercises with sets and reps, so a session plays like a coached class rather than a static plan. Eat Smart, Drink Smart hosts multiple named diet programs (8-week weight loss, HCG, bariatric, low-carb, Mediterranean, intermittent fasting and more); each one opens onto the same four-screen structure — Introduction, Approved Foods, Non-Approved Foods, Diet Meal Plan — so once a user learns one program, every other program reads the same way.

The Nutrition Diary pairs a plain food search with a running Diet Summary of calories, carbs, fat and protein consumed against goal, and FitGenie extends that same logging into a chat — send a photo, get the nutrition back, no manual search. The Meditation Library groups ten guided categories (Mindful Mornings, Stress Relief, Breathing Techniques, Deep Sleep Meditations and more) as a single flat, scannable list rather than a nested browse tree.
