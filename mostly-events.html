Case study

Mostly Events

A recruitment app built only for the events industry — where one event post staffs a whole crew. A décor lead, servers, a coordinator, a photographer, all from one listing. I designed both sides solo at Developer Bazaar: the recruiter product for event companies and the job-seeker app for freelancers.

Project Scope

Design the job-seeker and recruiter sides of Mostly Events end to end — from requirements with the product team through wireframes, high-fidelity screens and usability testing. Native mobile, built for how event companies actually staff a wedding: many roles, many rates, one post.

Benchmarked against Naukri, Indeed and gig-work apps. Delivered user flows, screens and the shared component library the team assembled from, with hand-off to engineering.

Problem Statement

Generic job boards assume one listing is one role, one salary, one Apply button. Event staffing isn't like that — a single wedding needs a planner, a decorator, servers and a photographer, usually for one day, often hired separately.

Recruiters were forced to post five listings for one event; freelancers couldn't judge their odds for the specific role they wanted. And a full-time hospitality manager and a wedding-day server have completely different needs — salary vs. day-rate, a formal process vs. verify-in-a-day. Goal: make one event post carry a whole crew, and give each side the right amount of structure.

Research & Ideation

I mapped requirements with the product team, then pulled apart the incumbents — Naukri, Indeed, WorkIndia and a few gig apps — to see what they optimise for and where event work falls through the cracks. Three findings drove the design:

Job boards optimise for salary, but gig workers weigh day rate, vacancy count, distance and speed of payment. A gig CV isn't a CV — event freelancers work company-to-company with no single employer history. Trust runs both ways — an employer needs a fast signal, a freelancer needs the company to be real.

From there I ran ideation across both sides — flows, sketches and low-fi wireframes — validating the “event as the unit” model before high-fidelity. The recruiter posting form and the job-seeker listing were designed together, so the same structure reads correctly from both directions.

The core decision

One event post. Many priced sub-roles.

Recruiter adds a Freelance Type → job-seeker sees distinct opportunities

Design & Results

I designed both apps end to end in Figma and built the shared component library the team assembles screens from — one typeface carrying the hierarchy, a mint-to-slate palette (#3BCAAE → #2F4858), and line illustrations for onboarding and empty states.

When a recruiter posts a freelance event job, they add a Freelance Type for each sub-role — Hospitality Management, F&B, a Shadow — each with its own day-rate range and vacancy count. On the job-seeker side that same post reads as several distinct opportunities. One post staffs the crew without becoming five posts to manage.

Recruiters verify with a business document and land on a dashboard built around one question — who do I still need to hire? — with a Good Fit / Maybe / Not A Fit assessment against each role. Job-seekers stay free, and see a plain-language status tracker that mirrors the recruiter's pipeline.

  • 4 post types, each with a purpose-built form: Full-Time, Freelancing, Internship, Project Manager.
