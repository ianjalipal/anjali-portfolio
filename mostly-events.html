One listing, many roles — staffing events shift by shift.

Mostly Events matches freelancers and job seekers to work in the events industry — weddings, decor, hospitality. Most job boards treat a listing as one role. Here, a single event can need a decorator, a server, and a coordinator at three different day-rates, all under one listing.

mostlyevents.com
01
01The Brief

Event staffing isn't one job — it's a crew.

A wedding needs a planner, a decorator, servers, a photographer — often for a single day, often through different agencies. Generic job boards force each of those into a separate listing with no shared context. Mostly Events treats the event itself as the unit: one listing, multiple freelance roles, each with its own rate and vacancy count.

△

Problem

Full-time hiring flows don't fit gig work. A wedding-day server and a full-time hospitality manager have completely different needs — day-rate vs. salary, one-off vs. ongoing, verified-in-a-day vs. a formal hiring process.

◇

Approach

Give freelance listings a structure full-time listings don't need — multiple sub-roles per event, per-role pay and vacancy counts — while keeping full-time roles on the simpler, familiar apply-directly path.

02The Application Flow

Two apply paths, one shared shape.

A full-time role is a direct apply. A freelance role adds one deliberate extra step: pick which specific role and rate you're applying for, since one listing can offer several.

03
03One Listing, Many Roles

The detail page that does double duty.

The freelancer variant of Job Detail adds a "Freelance Type" section beneath the same role description — each sub-role priced and counted independently, so a decorator and a server reading the same listing see different pay and different vacancy pressure.

₹Per-Role Day Rate

Each freelance sub-role — Hospitality Management, Food & Beverages, Shadow — carries its own ₹-per-day range, not one blended rate for the whole listing.

#Independent Vacancy Counts

"2 Vacancies" for decor, "1 Vacancy" for a shadow role — visible before applying, so a candidate can judge their odds for the specific role, not the listing as a whole.

?Screening Questions

Employers can attach custom pre-apply questions per listing — open-ended and yes/no — surfaced under a "Career Fit Assessment" heading before submission.

✓Save & Share

Bookmark and share icons sit on the detail page itself — a listing a candidate isn't ready to commit to yet doesn't have to be re-found later.

04A Portfolio, Not Just a Résumé

Built for people who work gig to gig.

Event freelancers rarely have a conventional single-employer résumé. The profile reflects that: an availability toggle, per-experience role tags (not just job titles), a dedicated portfolio link, social links, and a free-text "References / Companies" field for names a résumé template wouldn't have a slot for.

05Trust Before the First Shift

Verification as two short steps, not a wall.

Booking a stranger for a wedding-day shift is a real trust problem. KYC here is deliberately minimal — a photo and an ID upload, each independently confirmable — rather than a long form that would stall someone trying to pick up gig work quickly.
